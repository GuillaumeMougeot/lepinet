\subsection{Novelty is graded by taxonomic distance}\label{sec:results_openset}
On the withheld-taxa benchmark (\cref{sec:evaluation}), the flat head on EfficientNetV2-S read with the entropy rule detects an unseen species of a known genus with an AUROC of 0.887, an unseen genus with 0.963 and an unseen family with 0.973 (\cref{tab:openset_distance}).
The same order holds for all four heads on both backbones, and entropy is the best rule for every one of them on these images.
An unseen taxon is placed by the embedding as far from the known species as its lineage is from theirs, with or without hierarchical supervision: the open-set counterpart of the taxonomic alignment in \cref{fig:main_taxonomic_alignment}.
The hardest stratum is also the common field case, a new species in a familiar genus, and it is 7.9 to 9.8 points below the easiest, so a pooled AUROC flatters.

On trap images the near stratum collapses to between 0.528 and 0.606 for every head, against 0.843 to 0.900 for the same models on museum-style images.
It holds 962 crops of 13 species, 682 of them of one common trap species, \textit{Pleuroptya ruralis} (0.515 for the flat head); the mid stratum is 19 crops of two genera and cannot be read.
Shift lowers the logits of known species towards those of novel ones, so a species close to a known one passes as known: novelty detection is not robust to the domain, and a species-level answer on a trap image means ``this species, or a close relative the model never saw''.
