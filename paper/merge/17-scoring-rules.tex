\subsection{Open-set scoring rules do not transfer across scale or domain}\label{sec:results_rules}
The five rules of \cref{sec:evaluation} are computed from the same forward pass on the same images, and the best one changes three times.
\emph{With the head.} A flat cosine head is best read by entropy (0.899) and worst by the largest logit (0.626), a 27-point spread; with an angular margin the largest logit is best (0.907) and the spread is 1.2 points.
The margin moves the novelty signal from the shape of the logit vector into the size of its maximum, which makes the rule nearly irrelevant, at a cost of 1.0 point of species macro-$\text{F}_1$ for 0.8 points of AUROC.
\emph{With the scale.} The largest logit wins at 20\,M parameters and is beaten by MSP by 6 to 8 points at 198\,M; read with each model's best rule, the apparent loss of novelty detection with capacity falls from 7.7 to 1.6 points.
In a well-fitted embedding every image reaches a high cosine to some prototype, so the maximum saturates, and what still separates known from novel is whether one prototype dominates, which only MSP, entropy and the top-2 margin see.
\emph{With the domain.} On trap images the flat head's best rule becomes MSP, and the largest logit and energy fall below chance (0.42 and 0.39), because shift lowers every logit, known species included; B8's best rule changes from entropy to the top-2 margin.
Open-set results are therefore reported with the rule named, and the rule is re-selected whenever the model or the domain changes.
