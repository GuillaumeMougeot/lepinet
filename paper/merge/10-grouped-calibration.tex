Thresholds for the trap images need care, because the traps photograph each moth many times per night, so a random split of crops puts near-identical images on both sides.
For the second recipe we therefore split by capture group: a random 10\,\% of the (trap, night) groups calibrates the thresholds and the rest is scored.
Against a random split of crops, on a subset of 15,200 crops of 368 species, this changes calibrated species macro-$\text{F}_1$ by 1.0 point on average (between $-0.7$ and $+2.4$ over 92 models) and reverses no comparison between heads; the only pairs of models that change order are less than 1.7 points apart.
The random split used for the main comparison therefore does not favour one head over another.
