\subsection{The test fold is inside a foundation model's training set}\label{sec:results_contamination}
Global Lepidoptera and TreeOfLife-200M, the training set of BioCLIP-2 \textbf{(REF)}, are both drawn from GBIF.
Joined on the GBIF occurrence identifier, which identifies the photograph rather than the species, 93.3\,\% of our species, 65.4\,\% of our images and 413,865 of the 629,742 test images (65.7\,\%) are in BioCLIP-2's training data.
Every comparison with BioCLIP-2 on the test fold is therefore re-scored on the 35\,\% of the fold without an occurrence identifier in TreeOfLife-200M (217,856 images), for both arms.
A taxonomic check, whether the model has seen these species, would have reported the same overlap as harmless; the identifier join reads only provenance columns and runs on a laptop.
The trap images carry no GBIF identifiers and are not contaminated.

On the clean part of the fold, a frozen cosine probe on BioCLIP-2 scores 0.844, and fine-tuning it scores up to 0.915 (learning rate $10^{-5}$; 0.891 at $10^{-3}$), so a frozen probe understates the foundation model by 7 points; these runs used our earlier recipe, with a 50-image floor.
Self-trained on the trap images like our models (P5), the fine-tuned BioCLIP-2 matches the self-trained DINOv3 ConvNeXt-L on the probe set (0.622 against 0.627) and leads it on held-out species (0.488 against 0.431); with its backbone frozen during adaptation it reaches only 0.480 on the probe set.
The broader pretraining helps most on the species that adaptation did not cover.
