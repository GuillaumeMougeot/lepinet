\subsection{Which classes are averaged changes the comparison}\label{sec:results_conventions}
On a test fold drawn from the training distribution every species occurs and none is predicted without occurring, so averaging over the species present and over the species present or predicted (\cref{sec:metrics}) give identical scores; on the Global Lepidoptera test fold they agree exactly for every model we checked.
On the trap images they do not.
The 47,905 crops hold 486 species, and each model of the second recipe names between 1,725 and 2,540 species that are absent from them.
Counting those species lowers the species macro-$\text{F}_1$ from 0.55--0.68 to 0.09--0.15, and a calibrated threshold restores part of it (0.27--0.42) by removing most low-confidence predictions of absent species (\cref{tab:conventions}).

The heads differ in how many absent species they name, the conditional head the most on every backbone (2,266 to 2,540 at species), so the convention can change which head wins.
Of the 54 pairs of heads (three backbones, three ranks), four change order between conventions with non-overlapping three-seed ranges.
On EfficientNet-B0 at genus, the hierarchical head leads the independent head by 1.2 points when only the species present are averaged, trails it by 2.3 when predicted-only classes count, and leads it again by 1.5 after calibration; on EfficientNetV2-S at family, the hierarchical head leads the independent head by 2.8 points in the first convention and trails it by 2.3 in the last.
The main comparison and \cref{tab:heads_ranks} count predicted-only classes and report both zero and calibrated thresholds for this reason.
A macro-$\text{F}_1$ on evaluation data that cover a small part of the label set cannot be compared with another unless both state the class set and the threshold.
