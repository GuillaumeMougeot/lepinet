\subsection{Self-training on unlabelled trap images}\label{sec:results_selftraining}
\Cref{tab:selftraining} adds target-domain data to the recipe on EfficientNetV2-S with the BU head.
The teacher is the same model trained without trap images; its most confident predictions on the adaptation nights give 8,169 pseudo-labelled crops of 274 species, 98.7\,\% of them correct (\cref{sec:selftraining}).
Repeated to 2.5\,\% of the training rows, they raise the probe set from 0.366 to 0.593 and its held-out species from 0.301 to 0.365 (three-seed means without trap data), without a single trap label, and change the test fold by 0.07 points.

\emph{Dose.} One copy of each pseudo-labelled crop buys 45\,\% of the probe gain.
Repeating them beyond 2.5\,\% of the rows keeps the probe set within the seed spread (0.593 to 0.568) while the held-out species fall from 0.365 to 0.274, below the model without trap data: over-replication turns adaptation into memorisation of the pseudo-labelled species, visible only on the species that adaptation did not cover.

\emph{Labels against pseudo-labels.} True labels on a similarly gated set of 12,224 confidently predicted crops, at a similar dose (2.6\,\%), beat pseudo-labels on the probe set by 8.9 points; the set is larger and spans more species, so this bounds the price of label noise from above. On held-out species the two are within the seed spread.
Labels drawn over every species, as an annotation campaign would, gain a further 4 points on the probe set and lose all of self-training's gain on held-out species (0.305 against 0.301 without trap data).
Where the errors on held-out species land shows why: without trap data 11\,\% of them name a species of the adaptation set; one copy of the pseudo-labels raises this to 29\,\%, the chosen dose to 67\,\% and the largest to 82\,\%; true labels on the confident crops reach 87\,\% and labels over every species 97\,\%.
Replicated labels teach the model that anything on a trap sheet is one of the labelled species, which is the wrong lesson for a deployment that meets unknown species.

\emph{New traps.} Scoring each crop with a model whose pseudo-labels exclude its trap (twelve folds) or its site's four traps (three folds) keeps 86\,\% and 74\,\% of the probe gain, and leaves held-out species within the seed spread of full self-training.
Since self-training needs no labels, it can be repeated for each new deployment.

\emph{Capacity.} The 20\,M model with self-training is far above the 198\,M DINOv3 ConvNeXt-L without trap data on the probe set (0.593 against 0.457, three-seed mean) and below it on held-out species (0.365 against 0.381, within the spread).
Self-training the larger backbone in the same way gives 0.627 and 0.431.
