\subsection{Self-training on unlabelled trap images}\label{sec:results_selftraining}
\Cref{tab:selftraining} adds target-domain data to the recipe on EfficientNetV2-S with the BU head.
The teacher is the same model trained without trap images; its confident predictions on the adaptation nights give \textbf{N} pseudo-labelled crops of \textbf{N} species, \textbf{N}\,\% of them correct.
Repeated to 2.6\,\% of the training rows, they raise the probe set from 0.371 to 0.593 and its held-out species from 0.306 to 0.365, without a single trap label, and change the test fold by 0.07 points.

\emph{Dose.} One copy of each pseudo-labelled crop buys 44\,\% of the probe gain.
Repeating them beyond 2.6\,\% of the rows keeps the probe set within the seed spread (0.593 to 0.568) while the held-out species fall from 0.365 to 0.274, below the model without trap data: over-replication turns adaptation into memorisation of the pseudo-labelled species, visible only on the species that adaptation did not cover.

\emph{Labels against pseudo-labels.} On the same crops at the same dose, true labels beat pseudo-labels on the probe set by 8.9 points, which bounds the price of label noise from above; on held-out species the two are within the seed spread.
Labels drawn over every species, as an annotation campaign would, gain a further 4 points on the probe set and lose all of self-training's gain on held-out species (0.305 against 0.306 without trap data).

\emph{Capacity.} The 20\,M model with self-training is far above the 198\,M DINOv3 ConvNeXt-L without trap data on the probe set (0.593 against 0.457, three-seed mean) and below it on held-out species (0.365 against 0.381, within the spread).
