\subsection{Long-tail rebalancing}\label{sec:results_longtail}
Rebalancing is the first choice the recipe leaves to the dataset, and the two datasets answer it differently.
On Pl@ntNet-300K, whose test fold shares the training distribution, both rebalancing methods pay (\cref{tab:longtail}).
Square-root oversampling raises every head, by 1.3 to 9.7 points, and removes the top-down head's deficit; EMLA gives the best flat head (+4.8) but lowers the top-down head by 6.2 points.

\end{table}

On Lepidoptera EMLA buys 0.6 points on the test fold (BU, 0.894 against a three-seed mean of 0.887) and costs 3.9 points on the trap images, 5.5 on the probe set and 4.9 on its held-out species, each below all three seeds of the unweighted model.
The earlier recipe showed the same direction more sharply: ordered by how hard they push probability mass toward rare species, no rebalancing, square-root oversampling, balanced softmax \textbf{(REF: Ren et al. 2020; Menon et al. 2021)} and both together lose accuracy on the trap images at every step (0.349, 0.284, 0.224, 0.183), while the test fold does not order them.
What a model learns from a species' few dozen photographs is largely about those photographs (one photographer, background and camera); up-weighting them helps on a test fold that shares those artefacts and hurts on images that do not.

The two training sets differ in how heavy their tail is.
In Pl@ntNet-300K the median species has 22 training images, 62\,\% of species have fewer than 50, and the effective number of classes, $\exp(H)$ for the entropy $H$ of the class frequencies, is 19\,\% of the number of classes.
In Lepidoptera, whose corpus is capped at about 2,000 images per species, the median species has 163, 10\,\% have fewer than 50, and the effective number of classes is 54\,\%.
We therefore recommend square-root oversampling for training sets with a heavy tail, for instance when most classes have fewer than 50 images or the effective number of classes is below a third of the total, and no rebalancing when the model will be used under a domain shift.
Two datasets cannot place these thresholds precisely; they mark where each of our datasets falls.
