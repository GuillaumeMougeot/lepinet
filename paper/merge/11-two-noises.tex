Two sources of variation are reported separately.
Bootstrap resampling of the test images measures how much a score depends on which images were drawn; it is the uncertainty of the main comparison, whose models are trained once.
Retraining with another seed measures how much a score depends on the training run; the second recipe trains every configuration with three seeds and reports the range.
On the Global Lepidoptera test fold retraining is negligible: across twelve configurations (three backbones, four heads) the species macro-$\text{F}_1$ varies by a median of 0.07 points and at most 0.14.
On the trap images it is not: the calibrated species score varies by a median of 1.2 points and up to 2.7.
Calibration contributes to this, since the same configurations vary by only 0.2 to 0.5 points at zero threshold.
Bootstrap resampling of whole (trap, night) groups is of the same size on the trap images: a standard deviation of 0.25 to 0.30 points at zero threshold, against a three-seed range of 0.19 to 0.50.\footnote{Percentile intervals of this bootstrap sit above the point estimate, because a resample drops rare species that are only ever predicted and score zero; we therefore quote its standard deviation.}
For the second recipe, a difference between two configurations is claimed only when their three-seed ranges do not overlap, and on the trap images only when it also exceeds twice the bootstrap standard deviation.
On the trap images, a single training run per configuration, as in the main comparison, can therefore rank two heads whose difference is within the retraining spread.
