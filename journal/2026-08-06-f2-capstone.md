# F2: one representation, two cheap classifier stages, both axes

**Kind:** research · **Status:** **RESOLVED (2026-08-07). Both predictions correct, and the two
classifier stages compose.** One representation trained cleanly, then 2 + 2 epochs of frozen-trunk
classifier work, gives **in-distribution 0.9081 and probe 0.7541** — better in-distribution than any
end-to-end 20 M model and within 1.7 pt of the best on probe, for a fraction of the compute.

## The recipe, and why it had never been run

Every correction of the past week pushed work out of the representation and into the classifier:

| finding | what it moved |
|---|---|
| L5 | √-oversampling in the representation costs 2.88 pt under shift |
| L4 / cRT | rebalancing belongs in the classifier: +1.19 in-distribution, robustness kept |
| T2 | domain adaptation is 83 % available at the classifier |
| T2b | `domain_aug` is redundant once the classifier is adapted (+4.75 → +0.57) |
| centroids | even the prototype matrix is replaceable at inference (−0.29 pt) |

The recipe those imply — a clean representation plus stacked classifier stages — was never run,
because each finding arrived separately. F2 is that run.

## Result

| stage | in-distribution | probe | held-out species |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 — representation only, no oversampling, no `domain_aug` | 0.8949 | 0.6445 | — |
| L4 — + cRT classifier rebalancing (2 ep, frozen) | 0.9068 | 0.6539 | — |
| **F2 — + classifier adaptation (2 ep, frozen)** | **0.9081** | **0.7541** | **0.7594** |

**Predicted in-distribution 0.900–0.910 and probe 0.740–0.765; both landed inside** (0.9081, 0.7541).
The falsification line was in-distribution below 0.895, meaning the stages fight. Cleared by 1.3 pt.

**They compose cleanly.** cRT's in-distribution gain not only survives adaptation but is marginally
larger after it (+1.32 vs +1.19), and adaptation delivers its full shift gain on top (+10.02 pt on
probe). Neither stage undoes the other, which was the one thing that could have sunk the design.

## Against end-to-end training

| | in-distribution | probe | training |
|---|---|---|---|
| B3rep5x — full 5-epoch training with pseudo-labels | 0.9003 | **0.7706** | 5 ep, whole network |
| **F2** | **0.9081** | 0.7541 | 5 ep representation + 2 × 2 ep frozen |

F2 is **+0.78 pt better in-distribution** and **1.65 pt worse on probe**. So end-to-end still wins
the shifted axis by a real margin — the remaining gap is what the representation learns from target
pixels, which T2 already bounded at ~17 % of the total.

**But the compute picture is the point.** Once the representation exists, both classifier stages are
2 epochs on a frozen trunk — minutes, not hours — and they can be *re-run per deployment*. A new trap
does not need a new model, it needs a new classifier. That is a different operational story from
"retrain for 6.4 hours", and it is worth 1.65 pt.

## What this settles for the paper

**The project's through-line is now a single claim with four independent supports.** Coarse
supervision, long-tail rebalancing, domain adaptation, and the prototype matrix itself all turned out
to be classifier-stage concerns. F2 is the constructive version: assemble them and the recipe
simplifies rather than accumulating.

It also **retires two backlog items**. B2 (background suppression) and L3 (LDAM) are both
hand-authored *representational* interventions, and T2b showed adaptation subsumes that family —
`domain_aug` fell from +4.75 to +0.57 once the classifier was adapted. Neither is worth a run on this
evidence; both are recorded as de-prioritised rather than deleted, with this as the reason.

## What remains

- **The 198 M confirmation, once.** The recipe has now stopped moving, which is the condition the
  scale discipline set. This is the run it was reserved for.
- **A second adaptation round** on the frozen trunk: re-label with F2 rather than B4, ~30 minutes,
  and it targets exactly the 1.65 pt gap to end-to-end.
- **The joint-stage question.** F2 runs the two classifier stages sequentially. Whether one joint
  stage (rebalanced *and* adapted at once) does better is untested and cheap.

## G1: cRT confirms at 198 M, at half strength (2026-08-07)

Stage 1 of the scale confirmation. L5's representation, classifier rebalanced on a frozen trunk.

| | in-distribution | genus | family |
|---|---|---|---|
| L5 — representation only | 0.9055 | — | — |
| **G1 — + cRT** | **0.9112** | 0.9598 | 0.9791 |
| gain | **+0.57** | | |

**Predicted 0.912–0.920; landed 0.9112**, below the range by 0.0008 — near-miss, and the
falsification line (gain under +0.5 pt) cleared by 0.07. Both worth stating exactly rather than
rounding to "confirmed".

**It works, at roughly half the strength it had at 20 M** (+0.57 vs +1.19), and that is the fourth
instance of a pattern this project keeps meeting:

| intervention | 20 M | 198 M |
|---|---|---|
| `domain_aug`'s in-distribution cost | −0.36 | 0.00 |
| marginal supervision's shifted gain | +1.79 | +0.02 |
| self-training's dose effect (6 % → 2 %) | +3.36 | +0.02 |
| **cRT's in-distribution gain** | **+1.19** | **+0.57** |

All four **constrain or reweight** rather than adding information, and capacity absorbs them — cRT
only partially, which fits: rebalancing a classifier is a weaker constraint than an auxiliary loss,
so there is less for capacity to absorb. The single exception remains √-oversampling, which
*reshapes the data* and gets **worse** with scale (−1.52 → −2.88).

That gives the pattern a usable form: **ask whether an intervention adds information or merely
constrains the optimisation. Constraints weaken with capacity; changes to what the model sees do
not.** It has now predicted the sign correctly five times.

G2 — the adaptation stage — is running.

## G2: the staged recipe replicates at 198 M (2026-08-08)

Stage 2 of the scale confirmation — adaptation on G1's checkpoint, frozen trunk, 2 epochs.

| | in-distribution | probe | held-out sp. |
|---|---|---|---|
| L5 — representation only | 0.9055 | 0.7497 | 0.7641 |
| G1 — + cRT | 0.9112 | — | — |
| **G2 — + adaptation** | **0.9150** | **0.7648** | 0.7600 |
| B8 — end-to-end, same ingredients | 0.9060 | **0.7798** | 0.7816 |

**Predicted probe 0.780–0.800 with in-distribution above 0.910.** In-distribution held (0.9150);
probe landed at **0.7648, below the range** — the second time I have been optimistic about how much
of end-to-end's shifted advantage a frozen trunk recovers.

**But the trade-off replicates almost exactly across a 10× scale change**, which is the result worth
having:

| | staged vs end-to-end, in-distribution | staged vs end-to-end, probe |
|---|---|---|
| 20 M (F2 vs B3rep5x) | **+0.78** | **−1.65** |
| 198 M (G2 vs B8) | **+0.90** | **−1.50** |

Two independent scales, the same trade to within 0.15 pt on both axes. The staged recipe buys
in-distribution accuracy and gives up ~1.5 pt of shifted accuracy, and that exchange rate is a
property of the method rather than of the model size.

**So the scale confirmation succeeded even though the prediction missed.** What was being tested was
whether the classifier-stage recipe holds at 198 M; it does, with the same sign and nearly the same
magnitude on both axes. G2 is also the **best in-distribution model in the project** at 0.9150.

## F3: joint and sequential classifier stages are a near-wash (2026-08-08)

One classifier stage doing both jobs (rebalanced *and* adapted) against F2's two sequential ones,
from the same L0 representation.

| | in-distribution | probe | held-out sp. |
|---|---|---|---|
| F2 — sequential, 2 + 2 epochs | **0.9081** | **0.7541** | 0.7594 |
| **F3 — joint, 2 epochs** | 0.9061 | 0.7479 | **0.7703** |
| Δ | −0.20 | −0.62 | **+1.09** |

**Predicted in-distribution 0.902–0.910 and probe 0.745–0.760 — both landed inside.** But the
explicit falsification line was "either axis more than one floor below F2", i.e. probe below 0.7500,
and 0.7479 trips it by 0.21 pt. So: the prediction of "a wash" was right in range and wrong at its
own boundary, which is worth recording as a near-miss rather than a clean confirmation.

**The interesting part is the split.** Sequencing is worth +0.62 on probe; the joint stage is worth
**+1.09 on held-out species** (2.1× that floor). They are not equivalent — they trade the same way
the dose sweep did, with the cheaper option generalising slightly better to unseen taxa.

**Practical reading:** sequential for the shifted benchmark, joint for half the cost and better
transfer. Given they are within about a point either way, **joint is the better default** — one
stage, one set of hyperparameters, and the axis it wins is the one that measures genuine
generalisation.
